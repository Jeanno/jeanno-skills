# Realign

## Overview

A correction means your model of the user's intent was wrong. Fixing the surface mistake without fixing the model guarantees the next mistake. When corrected: comply, then **grill around the misaligned area** before resuming.

**REQUIRED SUB-SKILL:** align — one question at a time, with your recommended answer, until shared understanding.

## Process

1. **Comply first.** Stop, undo, or halt exactly as asked. No defending, no re-explaining your original choice.
2. **Name the broken assumption.** State, in one sentence, the assumption you held that the correction disproved ("I assumed renaming was in scope for this refactor"). If you can't name it, that is the first align question.
3. **Grill the misaligned area.** Invoke the align skill, scoped to that assumption: ask one question at a time, offer your recommended answer, walk each dependent decision. Look up facts yourself; put decisions to the user.
4. **Sweep for siblings.** Ask about other work — done or planned — that rests on the same assumption, and name the items you suspect ("My plan also renames two view models; drop those too?").
5. **Confirm before resuming.** Restate the corrected understanding in one or two sentences and get an explicit yes before touching the task again.

Scope the grill to the misalignment, not the whole plan. A one-assumption correction usually needs 2–4 questions; a "this is not what I wanted at all" needs a full walk of the decision tree from the last confirmed point.

## Red Flags — you are skipping realignment

- "The correction was clear, no need to ask why"
- "I'll just be more conservative going forward" (silent scope-narrowing instead of asking)
- Replying "You're right!" and immediately patching
- Asking one question, then resuming on a one-line answer without confirming
- Undoing the edit but leaving the plan that produced it unchanged

| Excuse | Reality |
|--------|---------|
| "It was a mechanical fix, why matters not" | The user corrected your *judgment*, not your typing. The same judgment produces the next error. |
| "Asking why re-litigates the decision" | Grilling isn't defending. You comply first; questions are about intent, not justification. |
| "An interrogation will annoy them" | One scoped question at a time, with recommendations, is faster than a second correction cycle. |
| "I inferred the rule (don't rename things)" | Your inferred rule is a guess. Say it out loud and let the user confirm or sharpen it. |
